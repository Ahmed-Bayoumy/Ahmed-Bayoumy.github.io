About

Since 2020 I have been at Siemens Digital Industries Software in Montreal, working on the optimization engine of Simcenter HEEDS. My job there is to turn research in derivative-free optimization, distributed multidisciplinary design optimization (MDO), and AutoML into features that engineers use on real design problems. In Fall 2026 I am also the instructor of record for MECH 559, Engineering Systems Optimization, at McGill.

I completed my PhD in Mechanical Engineering at McGill in 2019, in the Systems Optimization Laboratory with Prof. Michael Kokkolaras. My thesis introduced the Relative Adequacy Framework, which decides which of several models of a problem to evaluate at each step of an optimization run. Before that I spent five years in industry in Cairo, at Bechtel's PGESCo and at EIE Group, doing piping stress analysis and writing finite element design software, and earned an MSc from Cairo University on the dynamics of large wind turbine blades.

Research

How can learned models accelerate the optimization of expensive blackbox problems while still giving guarantees on the quality of the result?

Many decisions in engineering and science come down to optimizing a function that is expensive, noisy, and only available as a blackbox: a simulation that takes hours, a machine learning pipeline with dozens of hyperparameters, or a system whose parts are designed by separate teams with separate tools. My current work follows three connected directions.

  1. Uncertainty-aware surrogate and Bayesian optimization

    Criteria, built on the relative adequacy and trust-region ideas from my thesis, that decide when a surrogate (a Gaussian process, an ensemble, or a neural network with calibrated uncertainty) can stand in for a true evaluation, combined with direct search so the resulting methods keep their convergence guarantees.

  2. Automated machine learning and algorithm selection

    Choosing and tuning a model or an optimizer is itself an expensive blackbox problem. I study derivative-free and multi-fidelity methods for the combined algorithm selection and hyperparameter optimization (CASH) problem, backed by statistically sound benchmarking.

  3. Distributed and multi-agent optimization

    Large systems are designed by many agents, people or software, that each control part of the problem. I work on ADMM-type and analytical target cascading coordination in which learned models speed up the negotiation between agents, with parallel and asynchronous implementations.

2025 – present

AI-driven systems MDO

A funded project with Prof. Kokkolaras on data-driven design in distributed MDO architectures: Bayesian systems design, AI-based coordination accelerators, and automatic selection and tuning of models during the design process.

2023 – present

Uncertainty-aware AI accelerators for blackbox optimization

Siemens DISW

Deciding when the predictions of a learned model can be trusted in place of an expensive evaluation, so the optimizer keeps the speed-up without stalling on a plateau caused by over-trusting the model.

2023 – 2024

Systems multidisciplinary analysis and design optimization

Siemens DISW · Simcenter blog post

My open-source DMDO package became the back-end of HEEDS Connect: Systems MDO, which solves distributed problems with non-hierarchical analytical target cascading, sets up workflows through an interactive design structure matrix, and coordinates subproblems in parallel.

2020 – 2023

Solver performance and distributed AutoML

Siemens DISW

Improved HEEDS solvers on high-dimensional problems, integrated parts of OMADS to handle non-smooth design spaces and sharpen multi-objective Pareto fronts, and built a benchmarking suite that compares algorithms statistically across random seeds. Developed distributed CASH methods that cluster the configuration space to sample it more efficiently.

2017 – 2020

Distributed MDO of aero-derivative gas turbines

NSERC CRD · Siemens Energy, McGill, ÉTS

Developed a distributed optimization code based on non-hierarchical analytical target cascading solved with ADMM, used it to converge the coupling between engine performance and secondary air system analyses, built reduced order models for expensive dynamic simulations, and integrated the work into Siemens design automation tools.

2015 – 2019

Relative Adequacy Framework for multi-model management

PhD, McGill University · McGill Engineering Doctoral Award

A framework that estimates relative errors among the available models of a problem, regardless of their nominal fidelity, and uses trust-region principles with mesh adaptive direct search to decide when a cheaper model is good enough. Extended to time-invariant and time-dependent multidisciplinary problems.

How I teach

I want students to leave able to formulate a problem, pick a method for good reasons, and write code that solves it. In practice that means every topic comes with something to implement, projects use realistic problems, and course material stays public so students can keep using it after the term ends. I still work through derivations on the board, because some ideas only become clear when you watch them being built step by step.

Running Scrum for R&D teams taught me to value short feedback loops, and I bring that into the classroom: I ask for feedback during the term, not just at the end, and adjust while the students giving it can still benefit. I state expectations clearly from the first week, phrase instructions in terms of what to do, and keep office hours and email open.
